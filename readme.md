The Hercules Challenge is a co-design consortium challenge available for first-year engineers through the mandatory introductory class, "Introduction to Professional Engineering." The idea behind the Hercules Challenge is to introduce humanitarian engineering to students who might otherwise never consider it.

## IsoPod | Pill System

![Status](https://img.shields.io/badge/Status-WIP-2C2C2C?style=flat-square)
![MIT License](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-MIT-F3F4F4?style=flat-square&logoColor=black)
![EM](https://img.shields.io/badge/Physics-Electromagnetics-2C2C2C?style=flat-square&logo=physics&logoColor=87CEEB)

The `IsoPod` is a pill system built around an axial shake generator, meaning it relies on no external power. It has multi-compartment storage bays for pills. It does have a Li-Po battery to store power generated by the user's natural motion while moving or shaking on-purpose. This could allow it to use an e-ink display to show, via picture graphs, when each storage bay should be taken. It also could have a ring LED that alerts the user during the first minute of each notification.

In humanitarian engineering, systems often help compensate for deficits. `IsoPod` is another example of a device that does this. It addresses medication adherence by leveraging memory and consistency, two areas where machines excel. At the same time, it offers a solution for users in power-constrained environments.

The machine uses `Faraday's Law of Induction`, which states that the rate of change of flux linkage over time produces an `opposing voltage` to the direction of motion.
Using related rates from calculus II, we can see the two main components:

$$V_{\text{induced}} = -\frac{d\lambda}{dt} \implies -\frac{d\lambda}{dz} \cdot \frac{dz}{dt}$$

This implies there are two different rates we can manipulate to induce a voltage. Firstly, increasing the rate at which flux linkage over the z-axis changes, or secondly, increasing the rate at which the armature is moving along the z-axis. Both of these can be analytically approximated as:

$$\frac{dz}{dt} = A \omega \cos(\omega t + \phi)$$

$$\frac{d\lambda}{dz} = \frac{d}{dz} (\mu H \cos(z)) = -\mu H \sin(z)$$

> [!note]
> Flux linkage can be approximated analytically for intuition, while the final flux linkage is obtained by FEM.

So from these two rates, the three main variables are exposed, increasing the acceleration `A` & frequency `$\omega$` will increase induced voltage and hence energy production, but are constrained by the user's physical abilities. Whereas the permeability `$\mu$` and `H` will increase the induced voltage while generally not being constrained by the user's physical abilities.
```
TOP (N) (3mm)

↓ (Gap 30mm)

core (N-S|S-N|N-S|S-N|N-S|S-N|N-S|S-N) (80mm) 

↓ (Gap 30mm)

BOTTOM (N) (3mm)
```

Hence the topology above was chosen, the`TOP` and `BOTTOM` magnets create a restoring magnetic force, allowing the device to build velocity from oscillating the device with the user's forearm muscles. The `N-S|S-N` arrangement leads to spikes in the B field over the z-axis, hence increasing induced voltage and hence power output.
